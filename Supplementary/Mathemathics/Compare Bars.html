For grown-ups: how to sit with her

  1. Look before counting. Ask "What do you notice? Which side looks like more?" before any numbers.
  2. Match in pairs. One from each side, side by side. The leftovers are "how many more".
  3. Say it both ways. "7 is 3 more than 4" and "4 is 3 fewer than 7" are the same fact.
  4. Ask "who has more?" first in every word problem. Most mistakes come from adding or subtracting before deciding this.
  5. Use real things. Socks, spoons, toy cars. Ask "How could we make them the same?"

The detective steps for every word problem

  1. Who has more? Find the bigger one (more, older, taller, longer, costs more). It gets the top bar.
  2. Draw the bars. Put a ? on the part you don't know.
  3. Is the ? the whole bigger bar? Then add: smaller + difference.
  4. Is the ? anywhere else? Then subtract: take away from the bigger bar.

Watch out for tricky words

"Fewer" or "less" does not always mean subtract. "More" does not always mean add.

Javier saw 16 monkeys today. That is 3 fewer than last Saturday.

Today is the one with fewer. So last Saturday had more, and it's the bigger bar. To find a bigger bar, we add: 16 + 3 = 19.
